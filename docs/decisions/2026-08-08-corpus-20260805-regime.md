### Corroboration, and its falsifier (0.12)

WO-053 independently measured the 5-minute maximum as **0.4076%** using **overlapping** windows
(n = 2,084, stride 1 bar). This summary uses **non-overlapping** windows (n = 427, stride 5 bars)
and reaches **0.4076%** — the same value to every digit, by a different code path over a different
sample of windows.

The falsifier: two different window schemes over the same data need not agree on the maximum — the
non-overlapping scheme could easily have *missed* the peak window, reporting a lower max. It did
not. The medians do differ (0.0412% vs 0.0402%), which is what confirms the two samples are
genuinely different rather than the same computation run twice.

## THE SCOPE OF WO-053's VERDICT

WO-053 concluded that a minutes-horizon taker strategy at Tier 1 is **inoperable** — 0 trades, with
the round-trip cost 4× the largest move in the corpus and 39× the median.

**That verdict is scoped to this regime.** It is a true statement about a quiet market. It is
**not** a universal claim, and a future reader must not take it as one:

- A volatile regime produces larger moves. It would need moves **4× this corpus's maximum** merely
  to break even at Tier 1 — but this corpus cannot say how often such regimes occur, because it
  contains none.
- The 60-minute row extends the finding usefully: even at an hourly horizon the largest move was
  0.5388%, still **3× below** the round trip. So the death certificate is not narrowly about
  *minutes* — in this regime it covers everything up to an hour.
- It says nothing about maker economics, about horizons beyond an hour (unmeasurable here — see
  the WO-054 §4 segment-length finding), or about better fee tiers.

**Any future citation of WO-053's verdict must carry this regime with it.** A death certificate
issued in a quiet market is evidence about quiet markets.
